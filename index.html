FACTORING TOOLKIT / LESSON 01

Greatest Common Factor

The first thing to check, every single time.

← Back to Factoring Toolkit

Let's break it down.

Factoring out a GCF means finding the biggest factor that every term shares.

Example:
8x² + 12x

Step 1: Find what both terms share.

8 and 12 share a greatest common factor of 4. Both terms also contain x. So the GCF is 4x.

Step 2: Divide each term by 4x.

8x² ÷ 4x = 2x
12x ÷ 4x = 3

Step 3: Write the factored form.

4x(2x + 3)

Step 4: Check by distributing.

4x(2x + 3) = 8x² + 12x. It matches!

WHY I TEACH THIS WAY

I taught elementary math for more than a decade before moving into high school algebra.

That background shapes everything I create. When students struggle with factoring, equations, or functions, I know the problem isn't always the new skill. Sometimes it's a missing piece from years earlier.

I teach Algebra 1 and Algebra 2 now, and I bring that elementary-math perspective with me: break big ideas into smaller steps, use visuals, and explain the why, not just the procedure.

Solve with Mrs. Yost is where I share those explanations and resources with students, families, and fellow teachers.
